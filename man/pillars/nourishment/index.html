What Nourishment actually means

You are what you take in. All of it.

When people hear Nourishment, they think food. Macros. Meal prep. That's one input out of five, and often not the most damaging one.

In the MAN protocol, Nourishment covers everything that enters your system: what you eat, yes, but also what you watch, what you scroll, how long you sleep, what you give your attention to, and what your environment is silently feeding your nervous system all day.

All five inputs manipulate your biochemistry. Your biochemistry determines your mental state. Your mental state determines what's possible for you on any given day. The N pillar is about becoming conscious of all five, not just the obvious one.

The MAN definition
Nourishment is the conscious management of every input that reaches your system. Food, sleep, media, attention, and environment. Each one either builds or depletes the nervous system that runs everything else.

The three levers

How the N pillar actually operates.

You can't optimise all five inputs at once. The N pillar works through three distinct operating principles that let you make meaningful changes without rebuilding your entire life at once.

Lever 01

Observation before optimisation

You can't change what you haven't named. The first job of the N pillar is simple awareness: what are you actually taking in? Not what you think you are. What the log reveals. One week of honest tracking across all five inputs shows patterns you didn't know existed.

The observation that emerges without prompting is always the most useful one. Don't skip the logging phase.
Lever 02

Light management

Morning light is the most powerful free input available. First light exposure calibrates your circadian rhythm, governs cortisol timing, and sets the sequence for every hormone that follows through the day. It costs nothing. Most people skip it because it requires going outside before checking their phone.

Get outside within 30 minutes of waking. No sunglasses. Even on cloudy days, the light signal is sufficient.
Lever 03

Input boundary setting

Not all inputs can be optimised. Some need limits. Media, notifications, news, and social feeds are engineered to capture and hold attention. The boundary isn't about restriction for its own sake. It's about reclaiming the cognitive real estate that compounding attention spend quietly steals.

One boundary at a time. Pick the input depleting the most. Set one rule. Run it for two weeks. Then the next.
A practice to start this week

The nourishment audit.

Not a diet plan. Not a detox. A ten-minute inventory of what's actually entering your system across all five channels, and one honest observation from each.

✦ N pillar practice

Time: 10 minutes. End of day. No judgement, no prescription. Just observation.

Food
What did I actually eat today? Does the pattern support how I want to feel tomorrow?
Sleep
What time did I sleep last night? What do I know about why, if it wasn't enough?
Media
What content did I consume today? Did it leave me more or less regulated?
Attention
What did I actually give my focus to today? Was it chosen, or just where I drifted?
Environment
What was my physical space like today? What was it silently asking of my nervous system?
One observation per channel. Not a grade. Not a plan. Just the thing you noticed that you otherwise wouldn't have. The pattern across five days tells you where to start.
