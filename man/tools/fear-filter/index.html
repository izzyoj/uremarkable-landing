The Fear Filter

Three questions. Name the filter that's been running under your decisions. Once it's named, it loses its grip.

3 questions Under 2 minutes No account needed

It's not a courage problem.
It's a misidentified fear.

Most people treat holding back as a willpower failure. It isn't. It's a filter running in the background of every decision, installed before you had words for it. The filter doesn't disappear when you push through it. It disappears when you name it correctly.

Question 1 of 3 33%
Question 1

Name the thing you've been holding.

The unsent email. The idea you've had for months and never said. The thing you were about to say in the meeting. And didn't. Write it here in one sentence.

Question 2

Which script is it protecting?

Every fear serves a deeper script, usually installed before the age of twelve. Pick the one that feels closest to what's running underneath yours.

🛡
Safety
If I put this out there and it goes wrong, I'm exposed. Vulnerable. Something about that feels genuinely threatening, not just uncomfortable.
👥
Belonging
If this goes wrong, I might lose my place. The approval, the connection, the "in." There's something about being seen to fail that feels like losing the room.
⭐
Status
What I've built, the credibility, the reputation for knowing what I'm doing, that takes a hit if this goes wrong and people see it.
Question 3

What are you actually protecting?

If this went wrong and people saw it, what would feel most at risk?

🎯
The result
A setback. A wasted effort. Something that went wrong but could, eventually, be recovered from.
🪞
The version of me they hold
How I'm perceived. The image. The story about who I am in that person's head.
