Who he is

The man who reads behaviour for a living.

Chase Hughes has spent his career in behavioural work, including interrogation, negotiation and jury consulting, and now teaches people to read behaviour, their own included.

MAN draws on his interview on The Diary of a CEO, where he explains why adults keep running scripts they wrote as children. What follows is our summary of that interview, in our words.

The core insight

Three problems every boy solves.

Much of what you do as a man was a boy's answer to a boy's problem.

Our summary of his interview, not a direct quote

Hughes says every child works out three things, usually somewhere between about five and twelve: how to make and keep friends, how to stay safe, and how to earn rewards or approval. Whatever worked became part of who he thinks he is, and he carries it into adult life like a backpack he never chose to pack.

That helps explain how a capable man can do everything right and still feel empty. The script that kept the peace at home or won praise at school is still making his choices, long after the problem it solved has gone.

How MAN uses it

See the script, then choose.

Hughes is the See step in MAN, which comes before Be, Do and Have. Seeing the script doesn't change it by itself, but it shows you which old answer your biggest gap is still running, so you can choose a different rep.

The quiz brief offers three optional questions, one for each side of the triangle, and the Fear Filter asks whose approval you're weighing a decision against. Both stay optional, with the care line close by.

Friends

What did you have to do, or be, to make and keep friends? For a lot of men it was being agreeable, useful or easy to be around.

Safety

What did you do to feel safe at home? Keeping the peace, reading the room early, or staying out of the way are common answers.

Rewards

What did you have to do to earn praise or rewards? "Do everything right" is a common answer, and it's the script behind MAN's opening question.

Which pillars

Where Hughes sits in the protocol.

Mindset Action

Hughes sits mostly in Mindset, because the triangle is about the story you run about yourself. It reaches into Action too, since the script shows up in what you avoid doing. The daily rep, built on BJ Fogg's work, is how the change happens, and Hughes helps you see what needs changing first.
